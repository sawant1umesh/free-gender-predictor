One of the most frequent surprises for expectant parents using the [Chinese Gender Predictor](https://freegenderpredictor.com) is discovering that their **Chinese lunar age** (*xūsuì*, 虚岁) is 1 or even 2 years higher than their regular Western chronological age.

Understanding how lunar age works is essential for interpreting traditional charts correctly.

---

## What Is Chinese Lunar Age (*Xusui*, 虚岁)?

In modern Western society, age is measured chronologically from the moment of birth. You are 0 years old on the day you are born, and you turn 1 on your first birthday.

In traditional Chinese culture, however, age has historically been counted using the **nominal lunar age** system:
1. **Age 1 at Birth:** A newborn is considered 1 year old immediately upon birth, traditionally recognizing the roughly nine-month gestation period in the womb.
2. **Year Added at Chinese New Year:** Everyone's lunar age advances by +1 on the day of **Chinese New Year** (the Spring Festival), regardless of individual birthdays.

As a result, your traditional Chinese lunar age can be either **1 or 2 years greater** than your Western age.

---

## When Is Your Lunar Age +1 vs. +2?

Whether your lunar age is 1 year older or 2 years older depends on where your birthday falls relative to Chinese New Year in your birth year and conception year:

| Birth Situation | Lunar Age Offset |
|---|---|
| Born **after** Chinese New Year (mid-February to December) | **+1 year** higher than Western age |
| Born **before** Chinese New Year (January to early February) | **+2 years** higher than Western age |

Because Chinese New Year follows the lunisolar calendar, it moves every year between **January 21 and February 20**.

---

## The General Formula for Chinese Lunar Age

To calculate your lunar age for any event (such as conception):
$$\text{Lunar Age} = (\text{Lunar Event Year} - \text{Lunar Birth Year}) + 1$$

## Why Using Western Age Causes Lookup Errors

The imperial [Qing Gong Biao](https://freegenderpredictor.com/blog/qing-gong-biao-history) chart was calibrated specifically for traditional lunar ages between 18 and 45. 

Using Western chronological age shifts your lookup coordinate by 1 or 2 rows on the grid. Because adjacent rows often display alternating boy/girl predictions, entering Western age will frequently change your reading completely.
