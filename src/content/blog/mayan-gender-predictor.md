Beyond the famous Chinese Gender Chart, another ancient civilization developed its own unique method for guessing a baby's sex: the **Mayan Gender Predictor**.

Renowned for their advanced mathematics, astronomy, and calendar systems, the ancient Maya created a simple parity-based system that remains popular among expectant parents today.

## How the Mayan Gender Predictor Works

Unlike the Chinese calendar which requires complex lunar date conversions, the Mayan Gender Predictor relies on simple numerical parity (even vs. odd numbers).

The system compares two variables:
1. **The mother's age at conception** (in solar years).
2. **The month of conception** (numerical month 1 through 12).

### The Parity Rules

- **Both Even (Even Age + Even Month):** Predicts a **Girl**
- **Both Odd (Odd Age + Odd Month):** Predicts a **Girl**
- **One Even, One Odd (Even Age + Odd Month OR Odd Age + Even Month):** Predicts a **Boy**

## Mayan vs. Chinese Gender Prediction

While the Chinese system relies on lunar astronomy and royal Qing Dynasty charts, the Mayan system uses mathematical symmetry. 

Both methods offer fun early pregnancy activities, but neither method considers human biological sex chromosomes (X and Y).

## Medical Accuracy & Disclaimer

Medical research confirms that folk methods like the Mayan chart carry a natural 50% accuracy rate—no better than a coin toss. 

For reliable gender confirmation, parents should rely on clinical ultrasounds at 18-20 weeks or NIPT blood tests from week 10 onwards.
