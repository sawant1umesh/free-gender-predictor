The **Chinese Gender Predictor** is among the most famous and widely searched pregnancy traditions in the world. With claims online ranging from "ancient accuracy" to "over 90% reliable," it is natural for expecting parents to wonder: **How accurate is it really?**

In this guide, we review the scientific research, statistical evidence, and psychological factors behind this ancient folk tradition.

---

## The Short Answer: About 50%

Large-scale epidemiological studies that evaluated the Chinese Gender Chart against real hospital birth records have consistently demonstrated that its predictive accuracy is **approximately 50%** — equivalent to flipping a coin.

Because human births naturally yield roughly equal proportions of boys and girls (around 105 boys to 100 girls biologically), any random guessing method will be correct approximately 50% of the time.

### Key Scientific Studies
1. **Swedish Birth Registry Study (2010):** Researchers at the University of Michigan and Karolinska Institutet analyzed **2.8 million births** recorded in the Swedish Medical Birth Register between 1973 and 2006. The study tested the Chinese lunar calendar method against verified birth outcomes and concluded that the accuracy was **50.0%** — identical to chance.
2. **Journal of Obstetrics and Gynaecology Study:** Other independent clinical reviews evaluating hundreds of mother-infant pairs confirmed that the Qing Gong Biao chart provided no statistically significant prediction over random expectation.

## Why Do So Many People Believe the Chart Works?

If scientific studies prove that the accuracy is 50%, why do millions of parents swear by the chart? The answer lies in well-documented psychological and social dynamics:

### 1. Confirmation Bias
When an ancient chart correctly predicts a baby boy or girl, proud parents remember the prediction, discuss it with family, and post about it online. When the prediction is wrong, it is quickly forgotten or dismissed as an input error ("I must have entered the wrong lunar month").

### 2. Social Media Amplification
Viral posts titled *"The Chinese Gender Chart was right for all three of my kids!"* capture attention and get shared widely. Statistically, in a family of three children, the probability of guessing all three correctly by pure chance is $0.5 \times 0.5 \times 0.5 = 12.5\%$ — meaning one in every eight families will experience a "perfect streak" through pure probability alone.

## Fetal Sex Determination: What Science Tells Us

In human biology, fetal sex is determined at the precise instant of fertilization:
* **The Mother's Egg:** Always contributes an X chromosome.
* **The Father's Sperm:** Contributes either an X chromosome (yielding a girl, XX) or a Y chromosome (yielding a boy, XY).

Because maternal age and lunar calendar months have no influence over which microscopic sperm fertilizes the egg, calendar-based charts cannot biologically influence or predict fetal sex.
