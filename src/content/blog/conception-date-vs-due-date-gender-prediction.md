When expecting parents use an online gender prediction tool, one of the most common points of confusion is which date to enter: **conception date**, **estimated due date (EDD)**, or the **first day of the last menstrual period (LMP)**?

Understanding which date to enter is critical to applying traditional Chinese gender prediction methods accurately.

## The Short Answer: Always Use Conception Date

Our [Chinese Gender Predictor](https://freegenderpredictor.com) strictly requires the **estimated conception date** — not the due date or the last menstrual period date.

This requirement stems directly from the historical methodology of the **Qing Gong Biao** (清宫表), the original imperial Chinese Gender Chart. According to traditional records, the chart pairs the mother's Chinese lunar age at the exact time of conception with the astronomical Chinese lunar month in which fertilization took place.

### What Our Tool Requires
* **Mother's Date of Birth:** Gregorian calendar format (we convert this automatically to your traditional Chinese lunar age, *xusui*).
* **Estimated Conception Date:** The day fertilization most likely occurred (not delivery date, not LMP).

---

## Why Does the Date Type Matter So Much?

The Chinese Gender Chart evaluates the specific **Chinese lunar month** of conception. Because lunar months do not align with Gregorian calendar months, entering different dates will almost always result in conflicting chart coordinates.

Consider this example:
* A baby with an estimated due date of **January 15, 2026** was likely conceived around **April 23, 2025** (266 days earlier).
* April 2025 corresponds to Chinese Lunar Month 3 (Year of the Snake), whereas January 2026 corresponds to Lunar Month 12.
* Entering January instead of April looks up an entirely different column on the imperial chart, giving a completely arbitrary result.

## How to Accurately Estimate Your Conception Date

If you do not know the exact day conception occurred, you can estimate it using one of three medically standard approaches:

### 1. Early Ultrasound Dating (Most Reliable)
Your OB-GYN or midwife typically establishes an estimated due date during an early first-trimester dating ultrasound (between weeks 7 and 12). 

To find your estimated conception date:
$$\text{Conception Date} \approx \text{Estimated Due Date} - 266\text{ days (38 weeks)}$$

### 2. Known Ovulation Tracking
If you tracked ovulation using luteinizing hormone (LH) ovulation predictor kits (OPKs), basal body temperature (BBT) shifts, or cervical mucus monitoring:
* Conception almost always occurs within **12 to 24 hours** after ovulation.
* Use your peak positive ovulation test date as your estimated conception date.

### 3. Last Menstrual Period (LMP) Method
For women with regular 28-day menstrual cycles:
$$\text{Estimated Conception Date} \approx \text{First Day of Last Period} + 14\text{ days}$$

*Note:* If your cycles are irregular or longer/shorter than 28 days, this method is less precise. Using ovulation tracking or ultrasound dating is strongly recommended.

## What If My Estimated Date Is Off by a Few Days?

For the Chinese Gender Chart, what matters is the **Chinese lunar month**, not the individual calendar day. 

Because each lunar month spans approximately 29.5 days:
* If your conception occurred mid-month, an estimate error of 2 to 4 days will virtually always remain within the same lunar month, leaving the prediction unchanged.
* If conception occurred within 2 to 3 days of a **New Moon** (the boundary where one lunar month ends and the next begins), an error could shift your lunar month number.

If your dates fall close to a lunar month boundary, test both adjacent dates to see if the prediction changes.

## Why We Don't Accept Due Date Input Directly

Some automated websites ask users for a due date and silently subtract 280 days behind the scenes. While convenient, this automated shortcut introduces several potential errors:
1. Standard pregnancy calculations count 280 days (40 weeks) from the *last menstrual period*, but conception actually occurs around Day 14 (38 weeks / 266 days prior to birth).
2. Automated subtraction without accounting for cycle length creates systematic shifts near lunar boundaries.
3. Asking directly for the conception date gives parents full transparency and control over their input data.

## Medical Disclaimer

The Chinese Gender Predictor is an ancient cultural folklore tradition designed strictly for personal entertainment and family fun. It has no clinical validity.
