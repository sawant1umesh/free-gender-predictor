## What Is the Chinese Gender Calendar?

The Chinese Gender Calendar is a traditional chart that pairs two values — a mother's age calculated in the Chinese lunar calendar and the Chinese lunar month in which conception occurred — to produce a folkloric boy-or-girl prediction.

The chart itself is a simple grid. Maternal lunar ages (typically 18 through 45) run down one axis, and lunar months (1 through 12) run across the other. Each cell in the grid contains either "Boy" or "Girl."

The method is rooted in Chinese cultural tradition and is associated with the imperial *Qing Gong Biao* (a historical palace record, also discussed in our [Qing Gong Biao history guide](/blog/qing-gong-biao-history)). It has been passed down through generations and today circulates widely online. Its origins are part of folklore, and historians have not independently verified all claims about its age or provenance.

What it is not: a medical diagnostic tool. Fetal sex is determined at the moment of fertilization by chromosomes — X from the egg, and either X or Y from the sperm. Nothing about the mother's age or the calendar month can influence or predict which chromosome combination occurs.

## How Does the Chinese Gender Calendar Work?

The lookup process has four straightforward steps:

1. **Determine the mother's Chinese lunar age** at the time of conception.
2. **Determine the Chinese lunar month** in which conception occurred.
3. **Find the row** on the chart corresponding to the lunar age.
4. **Find the column** corresponding to the lunar conception month, and read the cell.

The challenge — and the most common source of errors — is steps 1 and 2. Both require converting Gregorian (Western) calendar dates into the Chinese lunisolar calendar, which does not align neatly with the standard 12-month solar year.

## Chinese Gender Calendar 2027: What Makes This Year Different

Every year of the traditional Chinese calendar begins on a different Gregorian date. Chinese New Year 2027 falls on **February 6, 2027**. This date is the essential dividing line that affects both the lunar age calculation and lunar month assignment for all conceptions in 2027.

The Chinese lunar year beginning on February 6, 2027 is the **2027 Chinese lunar year**, running through **January 25, 2028**. It is a regular 12-month lunar year and does not contain a leap (intercalary) month.

**Why the February 6 cutoff matters:**

- The Chinese lunar year beginning on February 6, 2027 is the 2027 Chinese lunar year. Do not incorrectly treat January 1–February 5, 2027 as part of the 2027 lunar year; that period belongs entirely to the preceding 2026 Chinese lunar year (Year of the Horse).
- A conception occurring on **January 20, 2027** falls within Chinese Lunar Year 2026, in its 12th lunar month — not in Lunar Year 2027.
- A conception occurring on or after **February 6, 2027** (for instance, February 10, 2027) falls within the **2027 Chinese lunar year**, in its 1st lunar month.
- Similarly, any mother born before February 6 in any given year has a lunar birth year corresponding to the preceding calendar year.

Getting these dates right is critical. Many generic online calculators simply add a fixed number to the Western age or assume January 1 starts the new lunar year, which ignores the Chinese New Year cutoff and frequently produces an incorrect result.

## How to Calculate Chinese Lunar Age for 2027

Chinese lunar age (*xusui*) differs from Western age in two fundamental ways:

- A newborn begins life at age **1**, not 0, because traditional culture counts the months in the womb.
- Age advances by **+1 on Chinese New Year**, not on individual birthdays.

The practical formula for use with the gender calendar is:

**Lunar Age = (Lunar Conception Year) minus (Lunar Birth Year) + 1**

**What does "lunar year" mean here?** Your Gregorian birth date's lunar year is determined by whether you were born before or after that year's Chinese New Year. If you were born in January before Chinese New Year, your lunar birth year is the previous calendar year.

## How Accurate Is the Chinese Gender Calendar?

This is perhaps the most important question to address directly.

The Chinese Gender Calendar has not been scientifically established as an accurate method of predicting a baby's sex. Because the traditional prediction has two possible outcomes, a result can appear correct by chance, but this does not demonstrate scientific predictive accuracy. Rigorous scientific studies comparing the chart against verified birth records have found no predictive advantage beyond what random chance would produce.

This makes biological sense. Fetal sex is determined at fertilization by which sperm cell reaches the egg first — carrying either an X or a Y chromosome. Neither the mother's lunar age nor the calendar month possesses any biological mechanism that could influence or determine which chromosome fertilizes the egg.

Why do some people believe it works? Mainly because of **confirmation bias**: when the prediction matches the baby's sex, it feels meaningful and gets shared. When it does not match, it is quickly forgotten or attributed to an input error. Because either result is equally possible by chance, correct predictions will always occur for a portion of families — and those are the ones people tend to remember and discuss.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Chinese Gender Calendar 2027?**
It refers to applying the traditional Chinese lunar gender chart to pregnancies conceived or due in 2027. The process is the same as any other year — you need your lunar age at conception and your lunar conception month — but Chinese New Year 2027 falls on February 6, 2027, which marks the start of the 2027 Chinese lunar year and serves as the key reference point for getting those inputs right.

**How does the Chinese Gender Calendar work?**
It cross-references the mother's Chinese lunar age at conception with the Chinese lunar month of conception on a traditional chart grid, producing a boy or girl folkloric prediction.

**Does the Chinese Gender Calendar use the mother's age?**
Yes — but specifically her Chinese lunar age at the time of conception, not her Western chronological age or her current age.

**What is Chinese lunar age?**
Chinese lunar age is a traditional age system where everyone starts at age 1 at birth and adds a year at each Chinese New Year, rather than on individual birthdays. It is typically 1 to 2 years higher than Western age. Learn more in our explainer: [Chinese Lunar Age Explained](/blog/chinese-lunar-age-explained).

**Is the Chinese Gender Calendar accurate?**
The Chinese Gender Calendar has not been scientifically established as an accurate method of predicting a baby's sex. Because the traditional prediction has two possible outcomes, a result can appear correct by chance, but this does not demonstrate scientific predictive accuracy. It is a cultural tradition, not a medical prediction method.

**Can the calendar predict a baby's sex?**
It produces a traditional folkloric prediction. Biologically, fetal sex is determined by chromosomes at fertilization and cannot be predicted by lunar calendar data.

**Is the Chinese Gender Calendar scientifically proven?**
No. Published scientific studies have consistently found its accuracy to be statistically equivalent to random guessing.

**Can I use the calendar for 2027 pregnancies?**
Yes — you can apply the method to any conception date in 2027 using the correct Chinese New Year date (February 6, 2027) to derive the appropriate lunar age and lunar month values. Dates from January 1 through February 5, 2027 belong to the 2026 lunar year, while dates from February 6, 2027 onward belong to the 2027 lunar year.

**What is the difference between lunar age and regular age?**
Western age starts at 0 and increments on your birthday. Chinese lunar age starts at 1 at birth and increments on Chinese New Year. The difference is typically 1 year, or 2 years for those born in January before Chinese New Year.
