Both the **Chinese Gender Predictor** and the **Mayan Gender Predictor** are traditional methods that people use to guess a baby's sex during pregnancy. They have been shared through cultural folklore for generations and remain popular online today.

While both methods aim to answer the same question—boy or girl?—they use very different calculation systems. The Chinese method is rooted in lunisolar calendar math, while the Mayan method relies on a simple even-and-odd number comparison. Because their rules differ, the same pregnancy information can produce different predictions depending on which method you use.

This comparison guide explains how each method works, where they differ, and what to expect when you try them side by side. Neither method has been shown through medical research to predict fetal sex better than chance, so both should be treated as fun entertainment rather than a scientific tool.

## How Does the Chinese Gender Predictor Work?

The [Chinese Gender Predictor](https://freegenderpredictor.com) is based on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar. Rather than using standard Western dates directly, it requires two converted values:

1. **The mother's Chinese lunar age at the time of conception.**
2. **The Chinese lunar month in which conception occurred.**

### Lunar Age Calculation

In the Chinese tradition, a person's age is counted differently than in Western culture. A newborn is considered one year old at birth, and everyone gains an additional year on Chinese New Year rather than on their individual birthday. This means the Chinese lunar age is typically one to two years older than the corresponding Western solar age.

To estimate your lunar age at conception:

- If your birthday fell **after** Chinese New Year in your birth year, add **1 year** to your Western age at conception.
- If your birthday fell **before** Chinese New Year in your birth year, add **2 years** to your Western age at conception.

### Lunar Month of Conception

The second input is the Chinese lunar month in which the baby was conceived. Because the Chinese lunar calendar does not align with the Gregorian calendar, Western conception dates must be converted to the corresponding lunar month for that specific year. This conversion can be tricky, especially near January or February when Chinese New Year shifts dates.

### The Chart Lookup

Once both values are determined, you find the intersection on the traditional chart: the mother's lunar age runs along one axis and the lunar conception month runs along the other. The box where they meet gives the prediction of Boy or Girl.

## How Does the Mayan Gender Predictor Work?

The [Mayan Gender Predictor](https://freegenderpredictor.com/blog/mayan-gender-predictor) takes a completely different approach. Instead of a calendar-based chart lookup, it uses a simple mathematical rule based on even and odd numbers.

### The Two Inputs

The Mayan method compares:

1. **The mother's age at conception** (in standard Western solar years).
2. **The month of conception** (the numerical month, 1 through 12).

### The Parity Rule

The prediction follows a straightforward comparison:

- **Both even** (even age + even month) → Predicts a **Girl**
- **Both odd** (odd age + odd month) → Predicts a **Girl**
- **One even, one odd** (even age + odd month or odd age + even month) → Predicts a **Boy**

The logic is based on whether the two numbers share the same parity. Unlike the Chinese method, the Mayan approach does not require any calendar conversion, lunar age adjustments, or chart lookup. You simply check whether each number is even or odd and compare them.

## Chinese vs. Mayan Gender Predictor: Key Differences

Now that both methods are explained individually, let's look at the specific areas where they differ most significantly.

### 1. Calendar System

The Chinese method depends on the **lunisolar calendar**, which tracks moon cycles and requires converting Gregorian dates into lunar values. The Mayan method uses the standard **Gregorian solar calendar** with no conversion needed.

This is the most fundamental difference. The Chinese method requires calendar literacy and date conversion, while the Mayan method works with the dates most people already use day to day.

### 2. Age Calculation

The Chinese method uses **lunar age**, which accounts for gestation at birth and the Chinese New Year age increment. This typically makes the lunar age one to two years higher than the Western age.

The Mayan method uses the mother's **standard Western solar age** at conception—no adjustments are needed.

### 3. Conception Information

For the Chinese method, you need the **lunar month** of conception (months 1 through 12 on the Chinese calendar). For the Mayan method, you need the **numerical month** of conception on the Gregorian calendar (January = 1, February = 2, and so on through December = 12).

### 4. Calculation Method

The Chinese method uses a **fixed lookup grid**. Each combination of lunar age and lunar month has a predetermined Boy or Girl result stored in the traditional chart.

The Mayan method uses a **parity comparison**. There is no chart to look up—just a rule that compares whether two numbers are even or odd.

### 5. Traditional Background

The Chinese Gender Predictor is associated with Qing Dynasty folklore and imperial court legends. The Mayan Gender Predictor draws on the mathematical and astronomical traditions of the ancient Maya civilization in Central America.

Both traditions carry cultural significance, though modern versions of these methods are primarily shared through online pregnancy communities rather than verified historical documents.

### 6. Result Interpretation

Despite using different inputs and rules, both methods produce the same type of output: a binary prediction of Boy or Girl. Neither method provides a probability range, confidence level, or alternative outcome—it is always a single definitive guess.

The mother's Western age is 26. Since she was born after Chinese New Year, add 1 year: **lunar age = 27**. The conception occurred in March, which must be converted to the corresponding Chinese lunar month for that year. Suppose the conversion yields **Lunar Month 3**.

On the traditional Chinese chart, the intersection of Lunar Age 27 and Lunar Month 3 gives a prediction. (The specific result depends on the exact traditional chart table.)

### Mayan Method Calculation

The mother's age is 26 (even). The conception month is 3 (odd). One number is even and one is odd, so the Mayan method predicts **Boy**.

### Why the Results May Differ

Even though both methods use the same underlying pregnancy information—age 26, conceived in March—they apply different rules. The Chinese method converts age to lunar age and requires lunar month conversion before looking up a fixed chart result. The Mayan method compares parity directly. Because the calculation paths are different, the predictions do not always agree.

## Which Method Is More Accurate?

This is one of the most common questions expectant parents ask, and the honest answer is that **neither method has been shown to be scientifically accurate**.

### What Research Shows

The most comprehensive study on the Chinese Gender Predictor analyzed birth records from 2.8 million births in Sweden between 1973 and 2006. The study found an overall accuracy rate of **50.2%**—statistically identical to a coin flip.

No comparable large-scale study has been conducted on the Mayan Gender Predictor, but given its simple parity-based rule, researchers and medical professionals expect a similar approximately 50% accuracy rate. With only two possible outcomes (Boy or Girl), any binary prediction method has a natural 50% chance of being correct by pure chance.

### Why Neither Method Is Medical

Biological sex is determined at fertilization by the father's sperm carrying either an X chromosome (resulting in a female, XX) or a Y chromosome (resulting in a male, XY). The mother's age and the time of year have no known biological influence on which sperm fertilizes the egg.

Traditional gender prediction methods are entertainment tools. For reliable fetal sex determination, medically validated methods such as ultrasound (weeks 18–22) or NIPT blood testing (from week 10) should be used.

## Why Can Chinese and Mayan Predictions Be Different?

It is completely normal for the Chinese and Mayan methods to give different results for the same pregnancy. This happens because:

1. **Different inputs:** The Chinese method requires lunar age and lunar month; the Mayan method uses solar age and numerical month.
2. **Different calculation rules:** A fixed chart lookup is not the same as an even/odd parity comparison.
3. **Different calendar systems:** Converting to the Chinese lunar calendar can shift the effective month value compared to using the Gregorian month directly.
4. **Different age values:** The Chinese lunar age is typically one to two years higher than the Western solar age used in the Mayan method.

These differences are expected and simply reflect the distinct traditions behind each method. Neither result should be treated as more reliable than the other.

## Are Chinese and Mayan Gender Predictors Scientific?

Both the Chinese Gender Predictor and the Mayan Gender Predictor are traditional folklore methods. They are not supported by medical research, clinical studies, or biological evidence.

Key points to keep in mind:

- **They are entertainment tools.** Both methods are designed for fun during pregnancy, not for medical diagnosis.
- **They are not medical tests.** They should never be used as a substitute for ultrasound, NIPT, or other clinically validated sex determination methods.
- **They should not influence healthcare decisions.** If you need to know your baby's sex for medical reasons, consult your healthcare provider.
- **Their predictions are based on tradition, not biology.** Maternal age and conception month have not been shown to influence fetal sex in any controlled scientific study.

Enjoying these traditional methods as a lighthearted pregnancy activity is perfectly fine. Just remember to keep expectations realistic and rely on medical professionals for accurate information.
